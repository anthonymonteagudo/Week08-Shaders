Rendering, shaders and materials

A renderer produces an image from scene data and a camera. Shaders perform calculations during that process. In a simple rasterized scene, triangles determine sample coverage and values such as UV coordinates reach fragment shading. Depth and blending influence visibility, while later processing affects the displayed color. Ned’s talk explains these connections in more detail.

A material defines surface behavior. In Unreal, its graph supplies values to outputs such as Emissive Color, Base Color or World Position Offset. A Material Instance inherits the parent graph and overrides exposed parameters. Changing a scalar or color is different from changing the parent graph or selecting a static-switch variant.

Vocabulary: a scalar is one number; RGB is a three-component color; UV is a two-component texture lookup address; a normal is a surface direction. A texture object refers to stored data, and a texture sample reads values from it. A type does not tell you the coordinate space or the intended meaning.

One tint calculation in three representations

Lesson 01 samples BaseTexture at UV, keeps RGB, then multiplies each component by Tint. A white multiplier preserves the sample. A red multiplier component below one reduces red. The input texture remains the same.

sample RGB = (0.8, 0.5, 0.2)
Tint       = (0.2, 0.8, 0.45)
result     = (0.16, 0.40, 0.09)

These are illustrative linear values. The display encoding affects what the result looks like. The approved slide example shows the source, multiplier and result together.

GLSL uses vec3 and texture. Unreal HLSL uses float3 and the host’s texture/sampler interface. The native graph uses Texture Sample RGB and Tint into Multiply. The math stays constant while the input/output wrapper changes. Read the exact source pair.

For the isolated UE example, use Surface / Opaque / Unlit and connect to Emissive Color. In a Default Lit material, tinted RGB can instead feed Base Color, which lighting and other surface properties then influence. Unlit still passes through the host’s display pipeline.

Try: show the untinted source, lower one channel, then restore it. In UE use MI_Tint_Graph, enable Tint under 02 Colors, and check that the mesh uses this instance. If nothing changes, check the assigned material and override checkbox before changing the code.

Color data: color RGB typically uses sRGB decoding. Numerical mask RGB should retain its numeric meaning rather than receive that decoding. Alpha is not sRGB-decoded. Check the texture’s purpose and sampler/compression settings.

Masks select values; blend mode controls coverage

A mask supplies a number to another operation. mix(A,B,m), lerp(A,B,m) and a Lerp node choose A at 0 and B at 1. Values between blend. Values outside the interval can extrapolate unless constrained. In lesson 02, smoothstep creates a mask from UV.x between 0.25 and 0.75. The plane remains opaque because the mask selects color.

The slide’s SmoothStep box summarizes the operation. The supplied native material expands it into arithmetic, including t*t*(3-2*t) after clamping the interval. More graph nodes do not imply a different result.

Unreal blend modeInput and observable behavior
OpaqueOpacity is not used for ordinary surface coverage.
MaskedOpacity Mask and a clip threshold keep or discard coverage.
TranslucentOpacity blends the surface contribution with the background. Setting it to 1 does not change the asset’s blend mode.

Check: does a gray Lerp Alpha automatically create transparency? No: identify where the signal connects and which material settings enable that behavior. Two Sided controls back-face rendering; it does not enable transparency.

Texture motion and water geometry

Lesson 03 samples a grayscale brush mask at changing coordinates. UV*Tiling changes the repeat scale. vec2(Seconds*Speed,0) adds a horizontal lookup offset. The sampled red channel blends ShadowColor and Tint. The source mask itself contains neither of those colors.

Controlled test: use Speed 0, 0.12 and -0.12 while Tiling stays 2 and colors remain fixed. Zero freezes the offset; the negative value reverses travel. The plane’s geometry remains fixed. If you increase the lookup coordinate, a feature can appear to travel the opposite way across the plane. Watch the feature instead of inferring its screen direction from the sign alone.

Vertex displacement needs enough vertices and matching shading normals. World Position Offset uses world-space offsets, normally in centimeters in this project, and does not automatically update collision. Extra mesh density does not generally fix texture filtering or resolution.

Light, surface color and roughness

A key light establishes the main form. Fill changes shadow visibility and contrast. A real rim light sits behind or to the side of the subject and depends on light placement and visibility. The gallery’s Default Lit examples use actual scene lights.

Base Color is a material input, not a promise of final screen RGB. Roughness changes how reflected light is distributed, which affects highlight shape and sharpness. Keep material color, camera, lights and exposure fixed when comparing roughness. Do not treat it as a universal brightness slider.

Check: if a color looks different in browser GLSL and Unreal, compare host color management, tone mapping, exposure and lighting before assuming that multiplication changed. The course pairs calculations and behavior; it does not claim pixel-identical output across hosts.

A visual target, a light signal and chosen colors

In the Wind Waker HD screenshot, identify broad colored regions and the tunic’s crisp shadow boundary. This visual analysis suggests a classroom experiment. It does not identify Nintendo’s shader code. The HD edition includes other lighting and image effects.

In lesson 04, N is a surface normal and L points toward a chosen light direction. Normalize both in the same coordinate space. Their dot product measures alignment; clamping creates a 0–1 light signal. step(Threshold,light) creates a binary mask and mix chooses ShadowColor or Tint. At a higher Threshold, less of the surface qualifies for the light color.

UE lesson MI_04_toon_Graph calls the direction parameter L. The separate lighting playground calls its analogous control LightDirection. The browser currently supplies a fixed L and camera. Use Threshold to test the boundary. The Unlit study does not consume scene-light or shadow-map data.

The capture provenance for slide 52 identifies two bands on the left and two bands plus a rim on the right. A broad view-angle blend introduces gradients within the band regions. The smoother-looking areas do not mean the base shader changed to continuous shading. Treat the strong accent as an exaggerated demonstration and reduce it when it distracts from the bands.

Lesson 05 uses N and the view direction V to create a view-angle mask. RimPower changes width; EdgeStrength changes the blend toward EdgeColor. Higher positive power narrows the edge. A dark EdgeColor can darken it because this is a blend. A real rim light and this mask respond to different inputs. The artistic mask alone is not a complete physical Fresnel model.

Browser test: keep RimPower fixed and compare EdgeStrength 0 and 0.8. Then fix strength and compare RimPower 1 and 5. There is no camera-orbit control in this browser. Camera-movement tests belong in the Unreal viewport.

Optional lesson 06 adds a brush perturbation to the light signal before thresholds choose colors. At TextureInfluence 0 the boundaries are clean; at 0.08 they shift with the texture. The browser study has three palette colors; the separate Unreal playground has four. Keep that distinction when following the optional slides.
