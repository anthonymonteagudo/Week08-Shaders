Look development: appearance to shader controls

Wind Waker: read the shading

Look at the broad light and shadow regions on the tunic, the crisp boundary, and the green shadow color. These visible features give us specific shader controls to investigate.

Nintendo: The Wind Waker HD. Screenshot 01, Nintendo. This is visual analysis of the HD edition, not a claim about its shader implementation.

Translate the visible features

Value: broad light and dark regions describe form.

Boundary: threshold the light response to make a crisp transition.

Palette: blend two chosen colors instead of only darkening one color toward black.

Classroom calculation: normal dot light, clamp, threshold, color blend. The upcoming GLSL, HLSL and material-node slides show that same chain.

Two bands with a view-angle accent

Start with two regions. Adjust the light direction, threshold and palette.

This capture adds a broad view-angle blend to two bands. The blend creates gradients within the band regions. Reduce its influence when it competes with the intended color grouping.

Our exaggerated edge accent demonstrates a separate artistic choice. A Fresnel-style mask follows the camera; it is different from a real rim light. The Nintendo screenshot does not prove the use of either technique.

Try it

  1. Open lesson 04. Keep the light fixed and use two bands.
  2. Choose a light green and a darker green. Change the boundary and compare the region sizes.
  3. Open lesson 05 separately. Keep RimPower fixed and compare EdgeStrength 0 and 0.8; then compare RimPower 1 and 5. The browser camera is fixed. Use the Unreal viewport for a camera-movement test.
  4. Keep the edge accent only if it supports your visual goal. Save a baseline and one intentional variation.
