Equations you can connect to linear algebra

Dot product converts direction alignment into a number. Linear interpolation forms a weighted blend. Smoothstep shapes a transition with a cubic polynomial.

For each equation, label the inputs, predict three results, then compare the result in the shader.

The shader bag of tricks

Small operations become useful when you can combine them and inspect their outputs.

  1. Shader tricks 1 · Direction becomes a signal

    Check: both directions use the same space. L points toward the light.

  2. Shader tricks 2 · A mask chooses the blend

    Check: show the mask as grayscale before using it to mix colors.

  3. Shader tricks 3 · Shape a transition

    Check: a < b. Soft edges alone do not guarantee alias-free sampling.

  4. Shader tricks 4 · Coordinates become a shape

    Check: Width > 0. Stretching the coordinate space stretches the shape.

  5. Shader tricks 5 · Build and inspect small pieces

    Check: wrapping comes from the sampler. fract/frac creates seams at cell borders.

Display the coordinates or mask directly before judging the final color. Keep a working copy, then change one input at a time.
